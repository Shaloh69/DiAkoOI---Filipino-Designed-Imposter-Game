# Clue Authoring — House Style

§6a is that three authors *agreed*, and agreement you didn't participate in isn't

---

## 1. What "tight" actually means

The useful distinction comes from how semantic closeness is actually measured, and there
are two different things people mean by "related":

| | Definition | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Similarity** | Two things share the same **features** | *graffiti* and *frescos* — both are painting, both on walls |
| **Association** | Two things **co-occur** in the world but share no properties | *painter* and *frescos* — constantly found together, nothing physically in common |

**This is the whole tier system.**

A clue built on **shared features** gives the imposter attributes they can describe. They
can talk. A clue built on **association** gives them the neighbourhood and nothing to say
— they know they're near food, but they can't describe anything without guessing.

> **Tight = high feature overlap. Loose = association only.**

This is also why algorithmic category-derivation fails (`02-CONTENT-PH.md` §14): "a fruit"
is pure association. It tells the imposter where they are and hands them zero features.

---

## 2. The feature test — make it countable

Before writing any clue, **list the word's core features.** Aim for 4–6. These are the
things a player would actually say about it at the table.

Example — **Adobo**:
```
1. ulam (a viand, eaten with rice)
2. may toyo at suka (soy sauce + vinegar)
3. maasim-alat (sour-salty)
4. matagal lutuin / niluluto nang matagal
5. karne — manok o baboy
6. pang-araw-araw, nasa bahay lagi
```

Now count how many features each tier hands over:

| Tier | Features given | What the imposter can do |
|---|---|---|
| **Tight** | **3–4** | Produce 3+ clues without repeating. Survives a full lap comfortably |
| **Standard** | **2** | One safe clue, a shaky second, then dry |
| **Loose** | **1**, usually the category | One vague clue. After that they're improvising |

That's the operational rule. If you can't say how many features your clue conveys, the
clue isn't ready.

---

## 3. The two tests to run on every clue

**Test A — the stranger test.** Hand the clue to someone who doesn't know the word. Ask
them to produce clues *as if* they knew it.

- Three plausible clues → **tight**
- One good, second one shaky → **standard**
- One vague clue and then they're stuck → **loose**

**Test B — the lap test.** From `02-CONTENT-PH.md` §3:
- Tight should survive **three crew clues** before the imposter is exposed
- Loose is *expected* to fail by **lap two**

If a word doesn't behave that way in playtest, **retune the word, not the tier
definition.** The tiers are fixed; individual words get adjusted to fit them.

## 5. Register — how it should sound

**Natural Taglish, the way you'd actually describe it to a barkada.** Not formal Tagalog,
not translated English.

| | |
|---|---|
| ✅ | "somewhere you eat when nagmamadali" |
| ❌ | "a location for expedited dining" |
| ❌ | "isang lokasyon para sa mabilisang pagkain" |

**Agreed conventions — argue with these in the session:**

- **Start with "isang" for loose tier**, drop it for tight. Loose is naming a category
  ("isang pagkain"); tight is describing a thing ("ulam na may toyo").
- **Lowercase**, no final period. These render on a card, not in a paragraph.
- **Length target: 25–60 characters.** Hard cap 90 (the validator warns above that). Tight
  runs longer than loose naturally — that's fine and expected.
- **Comma-separated fragments beat full sentences.** "mainit na ulam sa plate, baboy,
  pang-pulutan" reads faster on a card than a grammatical sentence would.
- **English where Filipinos actually use English.** "girl group", "host", "dessert",
  "score" — forcing these into Tagalog sounds like a textbook.

## 6. Failure modes — check every clue against these

| Failure | Example | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| **Contains the word or a synonym** | "adobong manok" for *Adobo* | Validator rejects. Rewrite |
| **Association-only when you meant tight** | "kinakain sa handaan" for *Lechon* — where it appears, not what it is | Give features: hayop, buo, inihaw, malutong |
| **Only decodable if you know the answer** | "yung sinasabi ni Darna" | Cut. Clue must work from zero |
| **Tight and standard read the same** | Both "isang ulam na may sabaw" | Validator flags. One must give 2 more features |
| **Uniquely identifying detail** | "may 7 members na Korean" — that's just BTS | Broaden. Tight ≠ giveaway |
| **Untrue** | "matamis" for *Sisig* | Misdirection is the players' job, never the author's |
| **Manila-only** | Naming a Metro Manila-specific chain as if national | Retag `region`, or cut |
| **Too generational** | A 1970s-only reference in a non-retro topic | A 19-year-old and their tita should both have a shot |

## 7. Difficulty rating (1–5)

Separate from tier. Tier is *how much the imposter gets*; difficulty is *how hard the word
is for everyone*.

| Rating | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **1** | Everyone at any table knows it — Adobo, Jeepney, BTS |
| **2** | Most people know it — Sisig, Palengke, Vice Ganda |
| **3** | Common but not universal — Bibingka, Dinuguan, NewJeans |
| **4** | Needs some interest in the topic |
| **5** | Niche. Use sparingly — one or two per topic maximum |

**Log honestly.** A `1` that was hard to write clues for is probably a `3`. Difficulty
distribution per topic should skew low: roughly 40% at 1–2, 40% at 3, 20% at 4–5.

---

## 8. Working checklist

Per word:

```
[ ] List 4-6 core features first
[ ] Tight  = 3-4 features
[ ] Standard = 2 features
[ ] Loose  = 1 feature (category or association)
[ ] Stranger test on the tight clue — can they produce 3 clues?
[ ] Tight and standard are clearly different from each other
[ ] No clue contains the word or an obvious synonym
[ ] Every clue is TRUE
[ ] Register check — would you say this out loud to a friend?
[ ] Under 90 characters each
[ ] Difficulty logged honestly
[ ] Run the validator before committing
```

Commit in batches of ~20 words, not 60. A bad calibration is cheap to unwind at 20.
